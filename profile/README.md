**Frictia Works** is a small independent game development team formed by three people who do not always fit together naturally — but keep trying to make it work anyway.

The name **Frictia** comes from the idea of **friction**: different personalities, different ways of thinking, different tastes, and sometimes completely different approaches to making things.

We are not three perfectly matching pieces.

We are more like three puzzle pieces that probably came from different boxes, somehow being forced into the same project.

And strangely, it works.

## About Us

Frictia Works is an independent game development team focused on creating games, experiments, prototypes, tools, and whatever ideas we find interesting enough to build.

We are a team of three, each with different strengths and perspectives.

That difference is part of how we work.

Sometimes it creates friction.

Sometimes it creates better ideas.

Usually, it creates both.

## What We Make

Our projects may include:

* Independent games
* Small experimental games
* Game prototypes
* Game development tools
* Technical experiments
* Interactive projects
* Things that probably started as a joke and became an actual project

We do not limit ourselves to a specific genre, engine, platform, or visual style.

If an idea is interesting enough, we may try building it.

## The Name

**Frictia** is inspired by the word **friction**.

The identity of Frictia Works represents three different pieces trying to connect despite not being naturally compatible.

That idea is also reflected in our logo:

**Three mismatched pieces, still forming one shape.**

It represents how we work as a team — not by being identical, but by finding a way to make our differences fit together.

## Philosophy

We believe small teams do not need to behave like large studios.

We want to keep the freedom to experiment, make strange decisions, abandon bad ideas, rebuild things, and create projects simply because we think they are worth making.

Not every project needs to become a product.

Not every experiment needs to succeed.

Sometimes making something interesting is enough.
